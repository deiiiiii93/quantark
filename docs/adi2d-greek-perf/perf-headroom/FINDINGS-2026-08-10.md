# Remaining performance headroom — measured 2026-08-10

Profiles and micro-benchmarks in this directory. Everything below is measured on
this host, not projected. Nothing here is implemented.

## PDE march: the tridiagonal solve dominates

`cProfile` of one 1y put march (n_x=61, n_v=120, n_t=240, CS, semi_lagrangian):

| tottime | ncalls | where |
|---|---|---|
| 0.296 s | 960 | `tridiag.py:14(solve_tridiag_batch)` |
| 0.039 s | 86,908 | `numpy.ufunc.reduce` |
| 0.033 s | 86,907 | `_wrapreduction_any_all` |
| 0.019 s | 480 | `adi_core._A0` |
| 0.014 s | 482 | `adi_core._advect_v` (the new SL transport) |

`solve_tridiag_batch` is ~62% of march time, and the 87k reductions are its
`np.any(np.abs(denom) < _PIVOT_MIN)` check **inside** the forward sweep — one
Python-level reduction per row per solve.

**WS-A1 (hoist the guard, check once after the sweep) — measured, BITWISE:**

| n_sys × n | shipped | hoisted | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 61 × 120 | 0.427 ms | 0.280 ms | **1.52×** |
| 300 × 135 | 0.698 ms | 0.531 ms | **1.32×** |
| 600 × 270 | 2.084 ms | 1.782 ms | **1.17×** |

Confirms the earlier research's 1.3×. The gain shrinks as arrays grow because
the per-row Python overhead amortizes. WS-A2 (C kernel) was measured at 2–2.7×
in the earlier round and remains unmerged.

## SLV Monte Carlo: the leverage lookup is half the cost

`cProfile` of a 2-batch paired SLV reference (ordinary_decayed, 1024 paths):

| tottime | ncalls | where |
|---|---|---|
| 13.847 s | 6 | `snowball_vol_mc_engines.simulate` |
| **12.976 s** | **18,144** | **`slv/leverage.py:134(leverage)`** |
| 3.864 s | 36,294 | `numpy.searchsorted` (mostly inside `leverage`) |
| 1.856 s | 9,072 | `_advance_conditional_affine_spot` |
| 0.927 s | 90,954 | `_clip` |

`leverage(spot, t)` is called with a path-vector spot and a **scalar** t, then
broadcasts t across all paths and runs an `n_paths`-long `searchsorted` on
identical values, plus four 2-D fancy-index gathers. Inclusive cost ≈ 47% of MC
time.

**Hoisting the scalar-time lookup — measured, BITWISE** (same arithmetic order;
1-D row views instead of 2-D fancy indexing):

| n_paths | shipped | hoisted | speedup | bitwise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | 36.7 µs | 20.5 µs | **1.79×** | yes |
| 8192 | 187.6 µs | 94.2 µs | **1.99×** | yes |

Implied end-to-end: ~47% of MC time at ~1.99× ⇒ roughly **1.3× on the whole SLV
reference stack, bitwise**, needing no re-baselining.

Caution: blending the two time rows *before* the strike interpolation is faster
still but **not** bitwise (4.4e-16), because it reorders the interpolation. Do
not take that variant for a reference estimator without a re-baseline.

## Numba / vectorization assessment

The Heston QE kernel (`volmodels/heston/mc_kernel.py:171`) already loops over
*steps* with full-array NumPy ops inside, so paths are vectorized. Numba would
not remove a per-path Python loop (there isn't one); its value would be **fusing
the ~15 temporaries each step allocates**, i.e. a memory-bandwidth play. That is
plausible but unmeasured, and Numba is a new heavyweight dependency the perf
spec currently lists as a non-goal — a decision, not an obvious win.

Cheaper and already-quantified work should come first: the two bitwise hoists
above, then the C tridiagonal kernel.

## Not yet investigated

- `rate_curve.get_discount_factor` — 791,172 Python calls (cheap each, 0.10 s
  total, but the call count suggests a vectorizable loop).
- `_clip` at 90,954 calls inside the MC path.
- The SL transport builds a **dense** (n_v × n_v) matrix though only 4 entries
  per row are non-zero; at n_v=120 it is only 0.014 s so it does not matter yet,
  but the cost grows quadratically in n_v while a gather would grow linearly.
