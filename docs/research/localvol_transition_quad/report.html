VERDICT

Verdict

On the headline daily-KI Snowball, the CTMC transition QUAD runs the full risk run (PV, native delta/gamma, parallel LV vega, theta, rho, carry: eight valuations) in 0.46 s (1y) and 0.76 s (2y) against 1.95 s and 4.39 s for the production LocalVolSnowballPDESolver, each engine at its own cheapest configuration that meets the budget (PV within 1bp, every Greek within 1% or its floor) against a reference two independent methods agree on. All ratios quoted here were timed in one window. PV binds for both engines. On event-free products the advantage disappears: the vanilla put and the monthly up-and-out are a tie or a loss (0.90x and 0.78x). The production MC never meets the budget within 2^20 paths. Independently of speed, QUAD is now a second, differently built check on the local-vol PDE: they agree within 2% of the budget on every output of every case.

METHOD

Method

Kernel: a continuous-time Markov chain on a finite-volume grid in ln S (Mijatovic-Pistorius; Zhang-Li grid design). Each row's up and down rates make the discounted stock a martingale of the chain and match the local variance, so mass and the forward hold by construction. Between observation dates the chain advances by the exponential of the generator frozen at each piece's midpoint (Magnus order 2, pieces cut at surface pillars and at one trading day), evaluated to 1e-13 by a Chebyshev-Bessel series or, for stiff pieces, a Weideman-Talbot contour. The only time error is that midpoint freezing: measured 2e-6 on a 1y put at daily pieces (it was 1e-4 with one piece per 0.25y slab). KO and KI are cell edges with a symmetric cell pair around each; s0 is a cell centre, so native delta and gamma come off a uniform stencil. Every interval carries [1, S/S0] probes and refuses the grid if mass or forward drift.

Protocol: the reference is the production PDE refined to 6400x64 (3200x32 for the put and up-out), accepted only because its last refinement step and the refined QUAD both sit within 1/5 of the budget. Each engine then climbs a fixed ladder and keeps its cheapest configuration whose worst output is inside the budget; MC must clear |bias| + 2 SE. Timing: one process window, single-threaded BLAS, an untimed warm-up on tuned configurations and an untimed validation-mode pass of each timed QUAD configuration (all identity residuals <= 1.4e-13 in the kernel), then three cold repeats with the arm order alternated, fresh engine objects and empty caches every time.

DUPIRE

Dupire repricing gap

The Dupire surface does not reprice its input. European puts on the production LocalVolSurface sit +1.2 to +10.8 vol points above Black-Scholes at the input implied vol, worst at the shortest maturity. PDE and QUAD agree on these prices, so the gap is in the surface: its first Dupire row (ATM local vol 0.485 against an implied 0.285 at 8 days) is clamped flat back to t=0, and linear-in-vol time interpolation misses the implied forward variances by up to 0.04 between pillars. Every local-vol engine in the repository inherits this; it is reported here, not changed.

08
FINDINGS

Findings

  • Where the speed comes from. Not from a cheaper transition: on the event-free put and up-out the production PDE at 100 points x 1 step/day is as fast or faster. In the same tuning run the Snowball PDE at that grid costs about 6.9x the European PDE (1.36 s vs 0.20 s, eight valuations), while QUAD's Snowball costs about 1.9x its put at the same 250-cell grid (0.43 s vs 0.22 s); the difference is consistent with the Snowball PDE's per-event work on 252 daily KI dates and its second (knocked-in) state, which QUAD handles as whole-cell masks on a second channel. That attribution is not measured in isolation. The 2y result is not an artefact of the flat-extrapolated slab beyond the last pillar (57% of the life): with QUAD's slab rate cache off, the in-window speedup is 5.6x (0.78 s vs 4.39 s).
  • Margin to the budget. The cheapest qualifying QUAD configurations sit close to the budget (0.65 of it on the 1y Snowball, 0.94 on the 2y); the PDE's sit at 0.32 and 0.87. Requiring QUAD to sit within half the budget (350 cells for 1y at 0.40, 400 cells for 2y at 0.27) still leaves it 4.05x / 4.85x faster in the same window.
  • Kernel bake-off: the Markov chain wins. The CTMC with a Chebyshev exponential meets the budget at 400 cells in 0.058 s per base solve. The moment-matched Euler-Gaussian kernel with Richardson extrapolation, the most independent of the PDE, needs 3645 cells and 49.9 s and refuses three of four grids (forward-identity residuals). Analytic density expansions (Pagliarani-Pascucci, Ait-Sahalia) were excluded up front: the production surface is only piecewise bilinear, and an expansion is an approximation by construction.
  • Two bugs the gates caught. A time-ordering error (pillar pieces applied earliest-first in backward induction) was invisible on daily-event Snowballs and on constant surfaces and was caught only by the European self-convergence check against the production PDE (1.24e-3 off; 2e-6 after the fix). A sliver cell between the readout block and the KO pair set the Gershgorin bound and made every operator 2.6x dearer (0.175 to 0.068 s per valuation); the grid now refits rather than leave a cell under half its neighbours.
  • The Dupire surface does not reprice its input. European puts on the production LocalVolSurface sit +1.2 to +10.8 vol points above Black-Scholes at the input implied vol, worst at the shortest maturity. PDE and QUAD agree on these prices, so the gap is in the surface: its first Dupire row (ATM local vol 0.485 against an implied 0.285 at 8 days) is clamped flat back to t=0, and linear-in-vol time interpolation misses the implied forward variances by up to 0.04 between pillars. Every local-vol engine in the repository inherits this; it is reported here, not changed.
  • Monte Carlo cannot meet the budget. With central +/-1% spot bumps on common random numbers, the production LV MC's gamma at 2^20 paths has |bias| + 2 SE of 36-53x the budget; its standard error alone is 13-20x. Scaling that standard error by 1/sqrt(paths), 2 SE <= budget on gamma alone would need ~2^29.4 paths (1y) and ~2^30.7 (2y). MC is reported at its best achieved accuracy, not timed against the claim.
09
LIMITS

Limitations

  • One surface: the 2024-02-08 CSI1000 crash-bottom Dupire fixture with flat r = 2% and flat q (parity-implied at the longest pillar). Calmer surfaces were not run.
  • Flat r and q in the QUAD engine; no term structure, no continuous KI, no Phoenix / step-down / lifecycle (valuation before the first remaining observation only).
  • Parallel local-vol vega only (a +0.01 shift of the grid, surface never rebuilt); bucketed vega, where operator reuse would matter most, is not measured.
  • The baselines are the production engines through their public API; a PDE rewritten to amortise its per-event cost could narrow the gap.
  • Timings: a shared Apple-silicon machine with foreign processes active; all arms ran alternated in the same window. Repeat spread is <= 6% except one PDE up-out repeat (18%); medians are reported.
  • No compiled kernel: the CTMC runs in NumPy, one vectorised tridiagonal matvec per series term on a few hundred cells; a C kernel is an open lever whose gain is not measured here.
