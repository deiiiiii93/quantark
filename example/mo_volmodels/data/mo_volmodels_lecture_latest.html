1   Market data and the implied-vol surface

MO options are European, cash-settled on the CSI 1000 index — the clean setting the three models assume (no early exercise). But live quotes do not come with a risk-free rate or a dividend yield attached: we must recover the carry from the prices themselves.

1.1   Put-call parity: recovering r, the forward, and the carry

For a given expiry, no-arbitrage forces a model-free linear relation across strikes:

C(K) − P(K) = DF · (F − K)   // straight line in K: slope = −DF, intercept = DF·F

A single least-squares fit of C−P against K yields the discount factor DF (hence r = −ln(DF)/T) and the forward F (hence the carry q = r − ln(F/S₀)/T). Here is what the MO chain actually implies:

Reading the market. The forward sits well below spot and falls with maturity — a steep negative basis. The recovered carry q is large (often 10–20%), not because the index pays such dividends, but because CSI 1000 futures trade at a deep discount, driven largely by structured-product (snowball) hedging flows. Note also that r is weakly identified at short maturities: since DF≈1, r=−ln(DF)/T amplifies quote noise by 1/T. The forward (the x-intercept) is robust; the rate (the slope) is not.

1.2   OTM selection and call-equivalent inversion

Only out-of-the-money options carry clean volatility information — deep-ITM quotes are dominated by intrinsic value and go stale. We keep OTM puts below the forward and OTM calls above it, then convert each OTM put to its call-equivalent price via parity, C = P + DF·(F−K), so a single Black inverter handles both wings and the two smiles agree at the forward by construction (the no-arbitrage property Dupire needs). 2   Dupire Local Volatility

Dupire (1994) asks: is there a deterministic instantaneous volatility σLV(S,t) such that a one-factor diffusion dS = (r−q)S dt + σLV(S,t) S dW reproduces the entire observed surface of European prices? The answer is unique and given by the forward equation (in implied total variance w = σ²T, log-moneyness y = ln(K/F)):

σLV²(K,T) = ∂Tw ÷ [ 1 − (y/w)∂yw + ¼(−¼ − 1/w + y²/w²)(∂yw)² + ½∂yyw ]

The denominator is a butterfly no-arbitrage term; the numerator is a calendar no-arbitrage term. Both must be positive. On real quotes they often are not:

Butterfly arbitrage in raw data. Building Dupire directly from the MO surface raises a no-arbitrage rejection at wing and short-dated nodes — the second strike-derivative ∂yyw estimated on a coarse, bid/ask-quantized grid produces a negative denominator. The builder refuses to fabricate a local vol there; the pipeline explicitly opts into a small vol_floor to proceed. A production desk would instead fit an arbitrage-free smile (SVI/SABR) upstream and keep validation on.

Repricing every OTM option through the local-vol PDE and re-inverting gives an overall IV RMSE of 4.89 vol-points, largest at the short end where the smile is noisiest and the floor bites hardest. In the continuous, arbitrage-free limit Dupire reprices the surface exactly; the gap here is the price of using raw quotes without an arbitrage-free parameterization.

3   Heston Stochastic Volatility

Heston (1993) makes variance itself stochastic — a mean-reverting CIR process correlated with spot:

dS = (r−q)S dt + √v · S dW₁
dv = κ(θ − v) dt + σ√v dW₂,    d⟨W₁,W₂⟩ = ρ dt

Five parameters shape the whole surface: v₀ and θ set the level (spot and long-run variance), κ the term structure of mean reversion, σ the vol-of-vol (smile convexity), and ρ the spot/vol correlation (skew). European prices follow from the characteristic function by Fourier inversion (Lewis form), which is what the fast calibrator uses.

Overall smile fit: 2.21 vol-points across all 6 expiries — and unlike Dupire this is achieved by an arbitrage-free model that cannot manufacture butterfly violations.

Two calibration lessons. (1) Identification. With only a handful of maturities the smile pins the combination σ²/κ, not κ alone — an unconstrained fit sends κ to any ceiling for marginal gain. We regularize (cap κ) rather than overfit. (2) Feller & numerics. The unconstrained fit is deeply Feller-violated (2κθ ≪ σ², so variance can hit zero), and that same extreme σ makes the downstream ADI PDE mis-price by ~2 vol-points. Adding a Feller penalty keeps 2κθ/σ² ≈ 1, costing a little smile fit but making both the calibration and the PDE trustworthy — the calibration objective and the numerical scheme are not independent choices.

4   Heston-SLV: the leverage function

Local vol reprices the smile but has unrealistic (deterministic) dynamics; Heston has realistic dynamics but cannot fit every smile exactly. SLV unifies them by multiplying the Heston vol by a deterministic leverage L(S,t):

dS = (r−q)S dt + L(S,t)·√v · S dW₁   // L chosen so E[v | S=K]·L(K,t)² = σ_LV²(K,t)

The calibration condition (Gyöngy) says the SLV model matches the market local variance iff L(K,t)² = σLV²(K,t) / E[vt | St=K]. We solve it by the forward Fokker-Planck method: evolve the joint (S,v) density one step at a time and read off the conditional expectation. The resulting leverage ranges L ∈ [0.14, 1.70] (L=1 would be pure Heston):

Which model for which product? The SLV PDE reprices these European vanillas to 5.60 vol-points — not better than analytic Heston. That is expected, not a failure: the SLV PDE carries an inherent few-vol-point discretization bias (visible even on a trivial flat-vol test), and on raw arbitrage-tainted data SLV also inherits the floored local vol. SLV's value is not vanilla repricing — Heston already handles vanillas, exactly and cheaply. SLV earns its keep on exotics (barriers, autocallables, forward-starting and cliquet structures) where both the market smile and realistic forward-vol dynamics matter. The leverage surface above is the reusable deliverable.

The arc of the study. A flat-vol Black-Scholes leaves a 2.7 vol-point smile on the table. Local vol can in principle remove all of it, but raw-data butterfly arbitrage limits it in practice. Heston, arbitrage-free by construction and Feller-regularized for numerical health, fits the whole surface robustly to a few vol-points. SLV then layers a leverage surface on top so the model is simultaneously smile-consistent and stochastic — the right tool once you leave vanillas for exotics. The recurring theme: market data is not arbitrage-free, parameters are not fully identified, and the calibration objective must respect the numerics that will consume its output.
