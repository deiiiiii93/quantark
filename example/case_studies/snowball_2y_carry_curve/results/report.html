READ

Findings

What the numbers say, in order of what a desk would act on.

  • The flat q misprices the note. Under the default hold-forward tail the flat q(T_mat) = 7.15% values the note +44.18 bp of notional higher than the curve. A flat-q desk would solve a coupon 1.30% p.a. lower than 31.60%.
  • The curve puts q in the 2027 H1 dividend window. Zero q rises from 2.5% (IM2611) to 9.5% (IM2703) and 10.5% (IM2706), peaks at IM2706 and falls back to 9.1% at the last pillar. At 10 of the 10 first-year KO dates the curve's q is above the flat 7.15%, so its forwards are lower there: P(KO ≤ 1Y) 64.2% vs 66.6%, P(KI before KO) 35.4% vs 33.1%, expected life 0.920Y vs 0.883Y (curve vs flat). The note is worth less on the curve.
  • The mis-hedge is where, not how much. Spot delta differs from the flat model by 0.3–5.4% and parallel rho by 0.8–10.9% across the tails (the hold-forward pair: 2.5% and 5.0%). But 44% of the hold-forward parallel rho (42%–47% across tails) lives beyond the last quote, and the hold-forward pillar hedge is a calendar spread: +11.25 IM2706 lots against −31.26 MO2709 C−P pairs, which IM futures alone cannot hold.
  • The unquoted tail is the dominant model risk. With the coupon held, the tail convention alone moves the curve PV from −125.5 bp (hold zero carry yield) to +158.4 bp (zero carry past last node): a 284 bp range, larger than the largest flat-vs-curve gap (70.1 bp).
  • The marks matter through the lever. Swapping the MO pair to its close, or the whole chain to CFFEX settlements, moves the hold-forward gap between 38.2 and 59.6 bp (base 44.2 bp): the sign holds, the size is uncertain by up to 15.5 bp.

Caveats

  • One flat vol for every cell (no smile, no local or stochastic vol); r flat; vol is not the subject.
  • The coupon equivalent divides by each tail's own curve annuity.
  • Static Greeks only: the historical curve replay / hedging backtest is deferred.
  • Mark-inconsistency: IM closes and the MO settlement are mixed across the levered last segment (see 03).
