Correlation model risk vs method gap

Across the interior uniform-ρ points (5 names), moving ρ by ±0.1 moves the reference per-name delta by at most 1,032 CNY/1%, while the largest M1 gap per scenario runs 9,463–12,819 CNY/1%. The smallest of those gaps is 9× the widest band: the method gap is not a correlation-assumption artefact.

PV · fast model vs multi-name MC

Of 96 scenarios, 83 PV gaps are resolved: MM is above MC in 61 and below in 22. Every case where MM is below MC is a high strike (K/I ≥ 1.05, calls and puts; a call and a put at one strike share the PV gap by parity, up to MC noise in the forward). At or below ATM, MM is above MC in every resolved case.

So the error is a skew, not a level: a basket of lognormals with dispersed vols is more right-skewed than the lognormal matched to its first two moments. The fast model is rich on low strikes and downside puts and cheap on upside calls.

What the desk should do

  • (a) Never split by target weights mid-period. After TECH drifts ±30% inside a quarterly period, the target-weight split (M2) carries a residual σ of 46,791–49,452 CNY/day against 2,645–4,507 for the whole-index order (M1).
  • (b) The whole-index order (M1) is adequate when the stub is short. M1's residual σ is 118 CNY/day under daily and 590 under weekly rebalance, against 28,846 on fixed shares.
  • (c) For fixed-share or long-period CSIs, hedge per name from a multi-name model (M3 or REF). On the base 1Y ATM call M1 moves delta from the highest-vol name, TECH (σ 45%), to the lowest-vol name, GOLD (σ 10%): M1 puts 10,875 CNY/1% less delta on TECH than the reference. M3's residual is not resolved (< 132) CNY/day. The total delta notional is the same under M1, M2 and REF (Euler; the M1 gaps sum to 0 CNY/1%): only the split differs.
  • (d) The moment-matched per-name model (M5) is a cheap middle ground. Its residual σ on the base case is 11,234 CNY/day against M1's 28,846. It inherits the MM model's own Δ_I error (section 8). Among resolved M5 gaps in sweep 1, the largest relative gap is +64.4% of the reference delta on LC2 (−5 CNY/1%) on the 1M put K/I=0.80; the largest in cash is +1.8% of the reference delta on ETF (+2,295 CNY/1%) on the 1Y call K/I=1.05.
  • (e) Quote PV off the multi-name MC. The fast single-index (moment-matched) model is not safe for quoting baskets that mix equities with hedge-like or negatively correlated assets: it is +0.75% (+61,017 CNY) off on the base ATM call and +5.92% (+297,921 CNY) on the EQ/HEDGE basket at ρ = −1.0.

Why it works · the error lives only until the next rebalance date

1. After a rebalance, only the index level matters. At the next rebalance date t₁ the share counts reset to wi·I(t₁)/Si(t₁). From then on, each period's growth is Σ wi·Si(tk+1)/Si(tk): it depends on returns, not on price levels, and under GBM those returns are independent of everything before t₁. So IT = I(t₁)·G with G independent of the state at t₁, and

V(t) = E[ h(I(t₁)) ],   h(x) = e−r(T−t₁)·E[ f(x·G) ],   I(t₁) = Σ ni·Si(t₁) with today's ni

Today's individual prices can affect V only through the stub up to t₁. Everything after t₁ sees only the index level.

2. The exact error is a covariance over the stub. Differentiating each path, with ∂Si(t₁)/∂Si(t) = Si(t₁)/Si(t):

REFi − M1i = E[ h′(I(t₁)) · ( niSi(t₁) − wi·I(t₁) ) ]   (cash delta; w = current weights)

It sums to zero over the names, because the weights sum to one. That is the Euler identity: every method carries the same total delta, and only the split differs.

3. To first order in the stub τ = t₁ − t. Expand h′ around today's index. The drift of the weights cancels the Δ that a naive expansion leaves in the vol term, so the vol term carries gamma only:

REFi − M1i ≈ τ · wi · I · [ (βi − 1)·σI²·I·Γ + (q̄ − qi)·Δ ]
βi = (Σw)i / σI²,   σI² = wᵀΣw,   q̄ = wᵀq,   Δ, Γ = index delta and gamma
termwhat it explains

4. The limit. As the rebalance interval goes to zero the index becomes a constant mix, dI/I = Σ wi·dSi/Si. I is then itself a GBM with σI² = wᵀΣw, and ∂V/∂Si = VI·ni exactly: the whole-index order is no longer an approximation. Daily rebalancing is one trading day away from that limit. The same limit makes I exactly lognormal, so the fast model's PV error should shrink with it too. That is a prediction: section 8 measures PV on fixed-share baskets only.

Index rebalance vs hedge rebalance

The rebalance rule in this section is part of how the CSI itself is built: it is written in the index rulebook. It is not about how often the desk re-hedges. Re-hedging a fixed-share index every day does not close the gap. Each day the weight split is recomputed with the same mis-allocation, and only a correct per-name delta (M3) fixes it.

Index rebalance (what this section varies)Hedge rebalance (desk activity)
Who does itthe index calculation agent, following the CSI rulebookthe trading desk
What changesthe index's own share counts ni reset to wi·I/Sithe desk's hedge positions, to match the new deltas
Effect on the optionchanges what the underlying is: payoff, PV and deltasnone: only how closely the desk tracks the option
In this studyrebalance= on the basket: none · quarterly · monthly · weekly · dailynot simulated: deltas are compared at one point in time
  • Frequent index rebalancing (daily, weekly). One basket order sized by Δ_I (M1) is close to exact: M1 residual σ 118 CNY/day under daily rebalance. No per-name model is needed for deltas, and the single-index lognormal is close to right. The cost: the index trades often, and the desk replicating it makes the same trades. Rulebooks usually pass that cost to the client through a decrement d (0 in every sweep here), which makes the product less attractive.
  • Fixed shares or infrequent rebalancing (none, quarterly). Cheaper to run and easier to explain, but the desk needs a multi-name model and per-name deltas. With the whole-index order alone it carries 7,174 CNY/day of residual σ at quarterly and 28,846 with no rebalance, per 1e8 CNY.
  • Rebalance dates always need a trade. Whatever the rule, the index's share counts jump on its rebalance dates, so a per-name hedge must trade on those dates to stay aligned.
  • Next step. A hedge backtest with daily re-hedging, A-share lot sizes and trading costs would put one CNY cost on each rule, combining split error and turnover, and make the rebalance frequency a priceable design choice.

MM

PV and Δ_I: fast model vs multi-name MC

The moment-matched lognormal is the desk-style fast single-index model. On the base 1Y ATM call it misprices PV by +0.75% (+61,017 CNY) and its total delta by +8,207 CNY per 1% index move. Of 96 scenarios, 83 PV gaps are resolved: MM is above MC in 61 and below in 22. Every case where MM is below MC is a high strike (K/I ≥ 1.05, calls and puts; a call and a put at one strike share the PV gap by parity, up to MC noise in the forward). At or below ATM, MM is above MC in every resolved case. On the 1Y ATM call: 5 names +0.84% (+80,886 CNY) at ρ = +1.00 and +3.70% (+87,295 CNY) at ρ = −0.25; vol dispersion +0.19% (+13,757 CNY) at λ = 0.0 and +8.97% (+942,141 CNY) at λ = 2.0.

So the error is a skew, not a level: a basket of lognormals with dispersed vols is more right-skewed than the lognormal matched to its first two moments. The fast model is rich on low strikes and downside puts and cheap on upside calls.

CAVEATS

Caveats

  • Multiplicity. The report tests 2,325 per-name method gaps at 3 SE (1,687 resolved). If every true gap were zero, about 6.3 would still clear 3 SE by chance (two-sided rate 0.27%). A lone resolved gap of a few CNY is weak evidence; the findings rest on patterns across scenarios.
  • Stylised, synthetic basket. No desk data. Weights, vols, yields and correlations are illustrative.
  • GBM only. No smile, local or stochastic vol, no jumps. A smile would change both the reference and the fast model.
  • Price-return index. No total-return or net-total-return variants. The decrement is zero in every scenario record; only the sanity checks use one.
  • Correlation band. The band measures how sensitive the reference is to a ±shift of a uniform ρ. It is a model-risk yardstick, not a confidence interval, and it is not defined at the PSD floor or at ρ = 1.
  • Static deltas. This compares deltas at one instant; it is not a hedge backtest and carries no costs.
