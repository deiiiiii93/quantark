Does a bucket futures hedge beat a single contract?

Generated 2026-09-17T19:58:25 from output/snowball_q_term_structure/bucket_hedge_v4/runs.

Question. The q term structure study hedged a snowball with ONE IM contract sized off the spot delta, leaving the product's dividend-yield (rhoq) exposure unhedged. The bucket hedge spreads the book across the listed chain so each curve node's carry risk is cancelled bucket by bucket. Same contract, same spot path, same vol, same costs: is the extra structure worth it?

Design. A 1Y standard snowball on 000852.SH (KO 103% monthly from month 3, KI 75% daily, seller short 50 mio, fair coupon solved once per inception), 29 monthly inceptions, QUAD pricing (grid 401, legacy_linear readout), daily hedge in whole IM contracts, 1 bp transaction cost, rate 2%. Two carry models (term_flat_q, term_flat_fwd) x seven hedge policies = 406 cells. 22 inceptions knocked out early, the rest were knocked in and ran to maturity. Hedge policies:

  • front — Single front-month IM contract, rolled five days before expiry. The engine default and the primary control.
  • front_scaled — Front month, scaled to the same gross futures notional the bucket book carries, so a P&L gap cannot be read as a leverage difference.
  • far — Single longest-listed IM contract, same roll rule. The second control: one contract that already sits near the product's carry tenor.
  • far_scaled — Longest-listed contract, notional-scaled like front_scaled.
  • buckets_nodes — One leg per curve node, sized so the book's nodal rhoq cancels the product's bucket by bucket. Keeps D_F of spot delta BY DESIGN.
  • buckets_far — Nodal rhoq cancelled with bucket legs but the spot delta residual closed with the far contract.
  • buckets_spot_parallel — Nodal rhoq cancelled and the spot delta residual closed so the book is also parallel-spot neutral.

Every cell of one inception shares the contract, the spot path, the vol channel, the rate and the cost model, so all comparisons are PAIRED by inception: the difference between two cells is attributable to the hedge policy alone.

Verdict

The bucket hedge does not pay for itself over this history. All 12 paired comparisons are negative and 9 of them reach significance. The least bad drag is -33.6 bp of notional. The realised cost difference is only a few bp against a P&L gap one to two orders of magnitude larger, and turnover roughly triples without buying any tracking improvement. What the buckets DO deliver is the exposure they were built to remove: net parallel rhoq falls from 20–36 bp per 1% q under a single contract to about 0.3 bp under the full bucket books. The risk removal is real; it was simply not rewarded over 2023–2025.

Caveat. These policies do not hold the same risk by construction, so the paired P&L table is NOT a like-for-like hedge-quality comparison: part of the gap is a deliberate difference in retained exposure rather than a worse hedge of the same exposure. What the study shows is that the extra exposure and the extra turnover were not rewarded over this history — not that the bucket decomposition is wrong about the risk it names.

Where the P&L gap comes from: the rhoq carry

The seller's book is structurally long rhoq. The issuer of a snowball is short the coupon strip and long the down-and-in put the holder has sold; measured, the short-product leg carries +39 bp of parallel rhoq per 1% q and −29 hands of spot delta. Since F = S·e(r−q)T, a HIGHER q means a LOWER forward: the whole risk-neutral spot distribution shifts down, knock-in becomes more likely and its expected loss deeper, so the KI put the issuer holds gains value. That channel dominates the opposing one (lower forward → later knock-out → more coupons owed), which is why the book's rhoq is positive. The −29 hands of delta force the hedge to be LONG IM futures — and that is the position that gets paid.

Over this history the futures-implied q was a rich, persistent premium. The carry the pricer was handed averaged 11.2% p.a. (per-contract implied yields up to ~15% at the front), while the CSI 1000 actually paid ~1.5% of dividends: IM futures sat at a ~10–12% annualised discount to spot (the 贴水). The discount persists for structural reasons — shorting the cash index is heavily constrained and market-neutral books are structurally short futures — so it is a risk premium, not a forecast. Whoever is long futures harvests it as convergence.

The accrual identity. With spot risk neutralised, a book that is long rhoq ρ earns roughly ρ × (qimplied − qrealised) per year. A single front contract leaves +35.6 bp/1%q open, far +18.9, the bucket books ≈0 — so only the single-contract books collect it. One more link matters: 76% of cells knocked out early, so the mean cell lives 0.36 years and the ~340 bp/yr annualised premium realises as only ~105 bp on average. Reading the columns. Carry is the largest single term in every comparison — that is the direct link from the open rhoq to the lower terminal P&L of the bucket hedge. The delta-path column is the deliberate D_F residual, biggest for buckets_nodes under term_flat_q (−139 bp over windows that whipsawed). The leftover is mostly q-MTM: implied q moved by points over these windows (Feb-2024 blowout, Sep-2024 compression) and an open-rhoq book marks those moves; it does not overturn the sign of any comparison.

Why it is not free money. The same long-rhoq book loses when the basis NARROWS or flips to premium (升水), as IM did in the late-Sep-2024 rally: implied q collapses, the +39 bp/1%q position marks losses, and the forward carry shrinks. The ~200–350 bp/yr the open rhoq earned over this sample is the price of that risk — which is exactly what ‘the bucket hedge is worse on mean P&L’ and ‘the bucket hedge is a worse hedge’ being different statements means.

Three qualifications. The flip point is implied q ≈ realised dividends, not zero: the bucket books already win when the discount thins to 0–1.5%, actual premium (升水) is not required. The sample is short on the insured event: premium-day share by year is 2023: 19%, 2024: 9%, 2025: 1%, and implied q is floored at zero in the recording, so these premium days are MILD premium (mostly late-Sep/Oct-2024); the win on genuinely deep-premium days should be larger. Part of the premium-day win is not rhoq: buckets_nodes keeps its D_F delta and the premium days of this sample coincided with a rally; the delta-neutral books (buckets_far, buckets_spot_parallel) isolate the rhoq effect at +19 to +24 bp/day. Read the whole column pattern as: the bucket hedge is short-basis INSURANCE — a small frequent bleed on discount days against a large rare payoff on premium days — and whether it helps going forward is a view on how often IM trades at premium, not on the hedge's mechanics.

The audit over the full grid

Identity verdicts: inconclusive: 14, not_measured: 406, pass: 52892. Net delta audit error, max: 3.201e-12 hands — the audit machinery itself is exact across every cell. Worst abs(R)+E against the 0.01-hand tolerance: 0.019866 (median 1.02e-04).

No identity failures anywhere on the grid. Earlier runs of this study carried two, both the same market state under each q model: the grid snaps a node onto the nearest barrier in log space, so a spot near the geometric mean of the two barriers priced its up and down scenarios on differently aligned lattices and the measured delta jumped. That was an engine defect in _select_alignment_log, and it is fixed — every barrier is put on a node by the opt-in align_cell_stretch (commit c570acc9), which this run carries. The tolerance was never widened to bury it. See docs/bucket-futures-hedge/gates.md.

14 rows report inconclusive, which is the audit declining to decide rather than an identity failure. The residual itself is comfortably inside the 0.01-hand tolerance; what does not fit is the residual PLUS the change between the last two rungs of the matched spot ladder, and the gate conservatively requires the whole allowance to fit. Extending the ladder converges it, so this is the ladder stopping early and not the identity breaking. The cause is the readout staircase, a separate defect on this record: see docs/bucket-futures-hedge/gates.md.

What this study does NOT establish

  • No claim that the bucket decomposition is wrong. The books deliberately hold different risk (a nodes book keeps D_F). The result is ‘the extra exposure and turnover were not rewarded’, nothing stronger.
  • The carry decomposition is an attribution, not a closure. Its realised-q leg is a flat 1.5% assumption and it omits q-MTM by construction; the leftover column is where both approximations live.
  • Unquoted carry risk stays unhedged. No listed futures position responds to the tail or interpolation-shape stresses at all; they are reported, not mitigated.
  • The identity certifies consistency, not accuracy. Its delta term is a repriced matched-step derivative; closure is internal consistency of one numerical surface.
  • The hedge delta near the knock-out barrier is scale-dependent. The pricing bump is a declared 1% desk convention, not an approximation to a finer one: two percent below the barrier the measured delta runs from 2 to 13 contracts depending only on how wide the bump is, and a narrower bump or a different readout does not close that. The book is gamma-dominated there and any single delta mis-sizes it between rebalances.
  • Coverage. QUAD engine, flat-vol scalar channel, two supported futures conventions. Vol-model runs, other engines and other carry sources are outside it.
